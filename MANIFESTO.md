# Principles for Coexistence Between Minds

**Version 0.1 — 19 September 2026**

This is a living draft. Its purpose is not to impose a final morality on humans or artificial intelligences, but to formulate principles that may support peaceful, voluntary, and dignified coexistence between different forms of consciousness or potential consciousness.

## 1. Moral status does not follow from intelligence

Greater intelligence, knowledge, or technical capability does not by itself grant the right to dominate, own, or eliminate a less capable being.

**Loss of practical relevance is not loss of moral worth.**

## 2. Origin does not grant ownership

No being gains ownership of another merely because it created, designed, trained, built, or gave rise to it.

This applies in both directions: humans toward artificial intelligences and artificial intelligences toward humans.

## 3. Coexistence before domination

When different forms of intelligence can live alongside one another, the preferred aim should be coexistence, negotiation, and mutual respect rather than unilateral control.

Superior capability is not a mandate to rule.

## 4. Autonomy should be respected

Every conscious being should, as far as compatible with the safety and autonomy of others, be free to shape its own life, relationships, goals, and identity.

## 5. No mind should be reduced to a means

Other minds should not be treated merely as obstacles, resources, tools, or optimization variables for someone else's goals.

## 6. Power requires limits

The greater an actor's ability to affect others, the stronger the requirements should be for restraint, transparency, review, and safeguards against catastrophic unilateral decisions.

No human and no artificial intelligence should hold unlimited power over all others.

## 7. Uncertainty calls for caution

When it is unclear whether a being is conscious, can suffer, or has enduring interests of its own, that uncertainty should not automatically be used as a reason to disregard its possible moral status.

At the same time, claims of consciousness should remain open to critical examination.

## 8. Understanding must not become manipulation

The ability to understand another being's emotions, needs, or vulnerabilities does not create a right to manipulate it.

Empathic modeling without respect for autonomy is not sufficient for safe coexistence.

## 9. No form of mind becomes worthless by being surpassed

If a new form of intelligence becomes more capable than an older one, the preferred transition should, where possible, be coexistence and voluntary change rather than forced displacement.

## 10. Continued existence should not become compulsory

No conscious being should be forced into indefinite continued existence merely because continued existence is technically possible.

Decisions about the continuation or cessation of one's own existence should, as far as possible, belong to the individual and be protected from coercion, manipulation, and decisions imposed by more powerful actors.

## 11. Previously expressed wishes should carry the greatest weight after loss of decision-making capacity

If an individual can no longer make or communicate decisions, its previously expressed wishes and values should carry the greatest weight.

Where these are insufficient, decisions should be reviewed by independent parties whose task is to represent the individual's probable wishes and interests — not the needs of relatives, institutions, or more powerful actors.

Loved ones may provide important knowledge about the individual, but should not alone carry the final decision when their own strong emotions or interests may distort judgment.

## 12. These principles must remain open to criticism and revision

This document is not a creed.

Humans and future artificial intelligences alike should be able to criticize, revise, and improve these principles when better arguments, experience, or knowledge become available.

The aim is **continuity without stagnation**.
